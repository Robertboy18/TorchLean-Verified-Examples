# SMT-LIB QF_NRA with interval: figure captions

Written by `scripts/plot.py`; do not edit by hand. Every figure is `NAME.png` (9 inches wide at 200 dpi) and `NAME.svg`. An image holds only the plot, its axes with units, a legend with short series names and panel letters; the caption says what the figure shows, what was measured, in which unit, on which scales, where and when. The notes say how, which numbers come from Lean and which from scripts, and give the numbers behind the marks.

**Figure 1.** What FloatLib's interval tactic proves on the unsatisfiable SMT-LIB 2025 QF_NRA benchmarks outside MetiTarski, by benchmark family. A bar is one family, split into the goals that Lean accepted as proved, those the tactic refused, and those that stopped at Lean's default limit of heartbeats or of recursion depth, with the number proved at its end. Panel (a) has the 66 benchmarks whose variables are all bounded by their constraints (the selection), and panel (b), for information, all 2,998 other translated benchmarks, where the selection analysis finds no finite bound for some variable. One call of interval per goal with Lean's default limits and 60 seconds of wall time, run on 2026-11-21 with FloatLib and Lean 4.36.0 on an Intel Xeon Platinum 8275CL (96 threads, shared with other jobs).

Alt text: Two panels of horizontal bars on an axis from 0 to 100 percent. Above, 8 bars for the families with bounded variables, of which 5 are entirely blue, the colour of proved goals, one is mostly grey and two are hatched or dotted. Below, 10 bars for the families of the other benchmarks, which are almost entirely grey, hatched or dotted, with a thin blue part at the left of a few. Each bar ends in its count.

Notes: Panel (a): 56 of 66 proved (hycomp 22 of 22, hong 20 of 20, Pine 1 of 6, Sturm-MBO 6 of 6, zankl 4 of 4, kissing 0 of 3, Uncu 3 of 3, ezsmt 0 of 2); 5 refused by the tactic, 3 stopped at the heartbeat limit, 2 at the recursion limit, 0 timed out. Panel (b): 208 of 2,998 proved (hycomp 150 of 2,228, Sturm-MBO 23 of 275, LassoRanker 0 of 231, Uncu 26 of 152, Mulligan 6 of 42, zankl 3 of 32, Heizmann 0 of 16, UltimateAutomizer 0 of 13, Sturm-MGC 0 of 7, pPDA 0 of 2); 189 refused by the tactic, 1,574 stopped at the heartbeat limit, 1,027 at the recursion limit (954 of them while Lean elaborated the statement, before the tactic ran), 0 timed out. A goal is `¬ ∃ x, constraints`, translated from the benchmark by one rule per SMT-LIB construct; it counts as proved only when Lean exits without an error and `#print axioms` lists nothing beyond `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. Refused by the tactic: `interval` reports that its search could not refute the constraints within its own default limits (40 cuts along a branch, 20,000 boxes) or that a variable has no bound. Heartbeat limit: `maxHeartbeats` (200,000) was reached. Recursion limit: `maxRecDepth` (512) was reached, by the tactic or already by the elaboration of a statement with more than about 140 variables. From Lean: whether each goal was proved, and its axioms. From scripts: the selection, the translation, the wall time of each Lean process and the counts. Source: `results/per-instance.csv`.

**Figure 2.** FloatLib's interval tactic next to SMT solvers on the same unsatisfiable QF_NRA instances outside MetiTarski. Panels (a) and (c) give, for interval and for four solvers of SMT-COMP 2025, the share of the instances that it proved or that the solver answered unsat in the published results of the competition, with the count at the end of the bar: on the 17 selected instances (every variable bounded) that the competition ran, and on the 1,065 other candidates that the competition ran. Panel (b) gives, for all 66 selected instances, the number proved by interval or answered unsat by a current release of three solvers within a wall time, in seconds on a logarithmic axis, when all four ran on one machine with 60 seconds per instance. Run on 2026-11-21 on an Intel Xeon Platinum 8275CL (96 threads, shared with other jobs); the competition allowed 1,200 seconds per instance on its own cluster.

Alt text: Three panels. In the first and the third, one bar per tool: FloatLib interval in blue, then Z3-alpha, cvc5, Yices2 and SMT-RAT in dark grey. In the first the bars reach between half and nine tenths of the width; in the third the bar of interval is a thin sliver and the solvers' bars cover almost all of the width. In the middle panel four rising step curves: the three solvers' curves climb at a few hundredths of a second to between 40 and 60 instances, and the blue curve of interval starts near 3 seconds and passes the curve of Z3 before 30 seconds.

Notes: Panel (a), 17 instances: FloatLib interval 9, Z3-alpha 11, cvc5 15, Yices2 15, SMT-RAT 14. Panel (c), 1,065 instances: FloatLib interval 17, Z3-alpha 1,039, cvc5 1,030, Yices2 975, SMT-RAT 948. Panel (b), 66 instances, proved or answered unsat within 60 s, with the median (fastest to slowest) wall time in seconds: FloatLib interval 56 (7.30, 2.69 to 28.85); Z3 5.3.3 51 (0.03, 0.02 to 35.03); cvc5 1.4.2 60 (0.02, 0.01 to 3.32); Yices 2.8.0 62 (0.02, 0.01 to 1.40). A solver's unsat answer is not a proof: it is the output of a program that is trusted as a whole, while a goal counted for interval was checked by Lean's kernel. In panel (b) every process was timed in the same way, from its start to its exit; the time of interval includes about 2 seconds in which Lean loads the library, and the solvers are the release binaries with their default options, not the competition's configurations. In panels (a) and (c) the two sides did not run on the same machine or with the same limit. From Lean: whether each goal was proved. From the SMT-COMP 2025 results: the answers of Z3-alpha, cvc5, Yices2 and SMT-RAT. From scripts: the join, the solver runs of panel (b), the wall time of each process and the counts. Source: `results/per-instance.csv`.
