# LeanCert's benchmarks with interval: figure captions

Written by `scripts/plot.py`; do not edit by hand. Every figure is `NAME.png` (9 inches wide at 200 dpi) and `NAME.svg`. An image holds only the plot, its axes with units, a legend with short series names and panel letters; the caption says what the figure shows, what was measured, in which unit, on which scales, where and when. The notes say how, which numbers come from Lean and which from scripts, and give the numbers behind the marks.

**Figure 1.** Goals proved against time per goal, by FloatLib's `interval` and by LeanCert's own tactic on the same statements, each with one call. A curve counts the goals proved within the time on the horizontal axis; the time is that of the whole `theorem` command inside Lean (elaboration, the tactic and the kernel check), the median of two repetitions for a run under 20 s and one repetition otherwise, with both tools running side by side. Panel (a) holds the inequality goals of LeanCert's seven benchmark suites and of its two script benchmarks, panel (b) the inequalities, with six combinations of comparisons, that LeanCert's examples and tests prove with one tactic call. The time axis is logarithmic, a curve further left is faster and one that ends higher proves more. Measured on FloatLib 4.1.0 and LeanCert 44b323a ported to Lean 4.36.0 and Mathlib v4.36.0, on 2026-11-21 on an Intel Xeon Platinum 8275CL (96 threads, shared with other jobs), at most 7 Lean processes at once.

Alt text: Two panels with three rising step curves each on a logarithmic time axis from 0.004 to 300 seconds. In panel (a) the FloatLib curve ends at 144 of 147 goals, the kernel-checked LeanCert curve at 138 and the compiled-checker LeanCert curve at 139; in panel (b) they end at 197, 203 and 210 of 210 goals. The FloatLib curve rises furthest to the left in both panels.

Notes: A goal counts for a curve only when Lean accepted the proof; for FloatLib and for kernel-checked LeanCert `#print axioms` lists only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound, while LeanCert's compiled checker (`native_decide`, the route LeanCert ships as its default) adds one compiler axiom per proof and is drawn for reference only. A goal is counted at the first rung of its ladder that proves it (plain file and default options, then raised `maxRecDepth` and `maxHeartbeats`, then one fixed set of larger tactic options); the suite's README gives the counts per rung. Goals proved, median and longest time per goal: panel (a) LeanCert, compiled checker 139 of 147, 0.50 s, 192 s; panel (a) LeanCert, kernel-checked 138 of 147, 0.76 s, 189 s; panel (a) FloatLib interval 144 of 147, 0.07 s, 40 s; panel (b) LeanCert, compiled checker 210 of 210, 0.12 s, 37 s; panel (b) LeanCert, kernel-checked 203 of 210, 0.32 s, 45 s; panel (b) FloatLib interval 197 of 210, 0.05 s, 2 s. Five LeanCert times of panel (b), of three goals that take more than 20 s, are from the first run and not from a repetition. From Lean: every time (`IO.monoNanosNow` around the `theorem` command) and every verdict. From scripts: the medians, the counts and the drawing. Source: `results/goals.csv`.
