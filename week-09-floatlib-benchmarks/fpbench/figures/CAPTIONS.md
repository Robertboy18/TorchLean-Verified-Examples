# FPBench with roundoff: figure captions

Written by `scripts/plot.py`; do not edit by hand. Every figure is `NAME.png` (9 inches wide at 200 dpi) and `NAME.svg`. An image holds only the plot, its axes with units, a legend with short series names and panel letters; the caption says what the figure shows, what was measured, in which unit, on which scales, where and when. The notes say how, which numbers come from Lean and which from scripts, and give the numbers behind the marks.

**Figure 1.** What FloatLib's roundoff tactic proves on FPBench. A bar is one kind of theorem over the whole suite, split into the cases proved, not proved and not translated to Lean, with the number proved at its end: 272 cases, the 136 programs of twelve FPBench suite files in binary32 and in binary64. Run on 2026-11-20 with Lean 4.36.0 on an Intel Xeon Platinum 8275CL (96 threads, shared with other jobs); these are the suite runs recorded in the repository of FloatLib.

Alt text: Five horizontal bars on an axis from 0 to 100 percent, three for conditional theorems and two for numerical bounds, with a blue part for the proved cases that is about two thirds of the bar for the forward and closed conditional theorems and small for the others. Each bar ends in its count.

Notes: Each program is translated to a Lean program and given to `roundoff` once per kind of theorem. A conditional theorem keeps its premises; a numerical bound is a second Lean proof that discharges them on the program's input box and proves one proposed bound. Proved: 184 forward, 180 closed and 22 backward conditional theorems, 33 forward and 18 closed numerical bounds (forward, closed and backward are the three methods of `roundoff`); 60 seconds and 4 GB per Lean process. Not proved means refused, over the limits or, for a numerical bound, not attempted; not translated means the translator refuses the case. A count says what the tactic did on this suite, not what it does on all programs. From Lean: every proof and its axioms. From scripts: the translation and the counts. The counts are `benchmarks/results/suites/counts.csv` of FloatLib's repository (FPBench 2026-11-20T11:24:20Z to 2026-11-20T11:32:47Z); the library sources of that run differ from the commit `fc2fad1f` by one added corollary and the version number, and no tactic differs. Source: `results/counts.csv`.
